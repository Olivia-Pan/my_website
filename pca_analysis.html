Introduction:
e project dataset I used is about college admissions that tends to study the level of economic
segregation in diﬀerent universities. e dataset contains universities names, parental income in
percentiles, aend, and interaction variables like aend_sat, rel_apply_sat, and etc. I want to ex-
plore the question of:
What diﬀerent characteristics do students from each UC school have?
e explanatory variables I decide to include are:
1. par_income_bin which is the percentile of parent’s household income;
2. aend which stands for the fraction of students aending this university among all test_takers
with similar parental income.
3. rel_apply which shows given the students’ test scores, how much more or less likely are they
to apply to this school compared to average college.
4. rel_apply_sat: relative application rate for a speciﬁc test score band.
5. aend_instate: aendance rate for in state students.
6. aend_instate_sat: absolute estimates on a speciﬁc test score for in-state students. Only avail-
able for public schools.
7. aend_oostate: aendance rate for out of state students.
8. aend_oostate_sat: absolute estimates on a speciﬁc test score for out-of-state students. Only
available for public schools.
9. aend_sat: absolute aendance rate for speciﬁc test score band.
estion: What diﬀerent characteristics do students from each UC school have?
Approach:
In order to study what diﬀerent characteristics do students from each UC school have, I ran data
wrangling to ﬁlter out schools in the UC system. en, I selected some explanatory variables that
are signiﬁcant to school enrollment: par_income_bin, aend, rel_apply, rel_apply_sat, aend_in-
state, aend_instate_sat, aend_oostate, aend_oostate_sat, aend_sat. Aer that, I chose to do
PCA analysis becuase by using the loading as the element of eigenvector, I am able to see how
much each variable contributes to each principle component.
I ran PCA analysis aer ﬁltering out numeric columns and standardize each column. Aer naming
the PCA analysis pca_ﬁt, I created a rotation matrix of loadings.
e next step is creating a scaer plot of ﬁed PC2 versus ﬁed PC1 because scaerplot allows
me to get more information on how each observations in each school are at with PC1 being the
x axis and PC2 at the y axis. It allows me to deeper connect with the rotation matrix. I colored
the students from diﬀerent UC schools: with dark blue being the best school in UC system, which
1
is UCLA, through the lightest version of blue representing the 5th best school in UC system, UC
Irvine. Aer the 5th place, UC Santa Babara is colored light pink, and the worst school is colored
darker pink.
Lastly, I align the rotation matrix and scaer plot side by side for easier comparison and analysis.

Observing the scaer plot, we can notice that all the beer schools are aligned at the upper le
of the scaer plot, and the schools that do not perform as well are located at the middle right
corner of the scaerplot, forming a big cluster of observations. According to the rotation matrix,
the aend, aendance rate from out of state and in state, and aend_oostate_sat all point to the
upper le corner, and only parental income variable points to the upper right direction.
Combining ﬁndings from both plots, I ﬁnd that among all test takers with similar parental in-
come, students are likely to aend UCLA and UC Berkeley. e same applies to both in state and
out of state students: they tend to choose to aend UCLA and UC Berkeley. However, there is
something interesting: the variable aend_level_oostate_sat, which is the estimate of aendance
rate on a speciﬁc test score band for out-of-state students, points to the upper le corver as well.
But aend_instate_sat does not point to the same direction. is implies that the top 2 schools in
the UC system emphasize more heavily on out of state students’ SAT score compared to in state
students.
On the other hand, parental income seems to play a huge role in UC San Diego, UC Davis, UC
Irvine, UC Santa Barbara, UC Riverside, and UC Santa Cruz students’ characteristics, and it is the
only factor the PCA shows. To put it in another words, the students from these schools tend to
have beer parental incomes, and these schools do not emphasize on test scores that musch, and
they do not have a outstanding aendance rate.
